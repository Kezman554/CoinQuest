2026-08-20 — Session B: money and calendar services

app/services/money.py
  - Integer pence throughout. format_pence(450) -> "£4.50"; parse_pence
    accepts "4.50", "£4.50", "4", ".5", "1,234.56", "450p" and signs.
  - Parsing rejects a third decimal rather than rounding it: an amount that
    cannot be paid is a typing mistake, not a value to guess at. A float
    passed as an amount raises MoneyError rather than being coerced.

app/services/calendar.py
  - Every function takes the timezone as an argument. Nothing reads the
    system zone and nothing defaults to one; the container clock is UTC.
  - Period(start, end, tz) holds whole local days. start/end are inclusive
    as a person would say them; starts_at/ends_before are the instants, and
    are half-open so 23:59:59.7 cannot fall out of the week that owns it.
  - week_start, week_containing, week_containing_instant, current_week,
    day_containing, month_containing, month_bounds, local_date, today,
    start_of_day, elapsed.
  - The week runs Sunday to Saturday. Both British clock changes fall on a
    Sunday, so a London week boundary at midnight is never ambiguous.

One trap found and defended against: when two aware datetimes share a tzinfo
object, Python subtracts them by wall clock and ignores the zone, so the
October week measured 168 hours instead of 169. elapsed() and
Period.duration go via UTC, and a test asserts both the wrong answer and the
right one so the reason cannot be lost.

Tests: 93 pass. Covers the BST-to-GMT week (169h), the GMT-to-BST week
(167h), the 25- and 23-hour days, both halves of the repeated 01:30, a week
spanning a month end and a year end, month bounds opening in BST and closing
in GMT, and pence round trips over every penny to £2. The suite passes with
the system zone set to America/Los_Angeles.

No float appears in either module or their tests, other than the two passed
deliberately to format_pence and parse_pence to assert they are rejected.

2026-08-20 — Session C: schema and the first migration

Tables (app/models/): chore_definitions, chore_instances, weeks,
settlement_lines, waivers, earnings_ledger, savings_ledger.

The separation the scheme turns on:
  - A definition is a rule and will be edited. An instance is working data
    and may point at one freely.
  - A settled week keeps its own figures, and settlement_lines keep their own
    copy of the chore's name, category, cadence and amount as they stood at
    the moment of settlement. source_definition_id is provenance only,
    nullable, ON DELETE SET NULL, and never read to work out money.

Enforced by the database, not by the application remembering:
  - Triggers refuse any change to a closed week's figures or status, any
    update or delete on either ledger, and any edit to a settlement line.
    paid_at and deposited_pence stay writable, because payday follows
    settlement.
  - CHECKs: a week starts on a Sunday (strftime %w) and runs seven days; a
    closed week carries all four figures and they add up; a voided week pays
    zero; no amount anywhere is negative; only a withdrawal is negative in
    savings and the balance never goes below zero; a weekly_count chore
    states its count and no other cadence may; a waiver fills in the columns
    its scope allows; a reward states its reason.
  - Partial unique indexes: one instance per chore per day, or per chore per
    week for the week-scoped cadences.

Alembic initialised at app/migrations with the URL supplied from the
environment through app.config, never from alembic.ini. render_as_batch is on
because SQLite cannot alter a column. alembic check reports no drift, and
downgrade base leaves an empty file.

One conflict found and resolved: ON DELETE SET NULL on a settlement line's
provenance column fires an UPDATE, which the blanket append-only trigger
blocked — making a definition undeletable once settled, and the SET NULL dead
configuration. The trigger now freezes every column that carries the money or
the story and permits exactly one change: provenance being cleared, never
repointed.

Instants are stored UTC and returned aware via a UtcDateTime type; a naive
datetime is refused rather than assumed. Local days are separate Date columns,
computed in Europe/London by app.services.calendar.

Tests: 138 pass. Schema tests build the database by running the real migration
against an empty file, not create_all, because the triggers exist only in the
migration. The central case asserts that renaming, repricing, recategorising
and withdrawing a chore leaves a settled week's stored figures untouched, and
that deleting the definition outright does too.

2026-08-20 — Session D: generating a week's assessable instances

app/services/instances.py. Pure: it reads no clock, writes nothing, and takes
a Period, the definitions and the waivers.

  - DAILY produces one instance per day the week contains — seven, including
    in the 169-hour October week.
  - WEEKLY_COUNT produces n instances tied to no day, numbered 1..n so each
    is separately claimable.
  - WEEKLY_CONDITION produces no instance at all. It appears in the plan as a
    deferred judgement, made once at settlement: a condition held all week
    cannot be assessed before the week has finished happening.
  - ONE_OFF and EVENT are not derived from a week. Both are created by a
    parent when they happen and neither is predictable from a definition.

Waivers: a waived day removes that day's daily instances; a chore waived for
the week is removed whatever its cadence; a day waiver outside the week or a
chore waiver for another week is ignored. A week-long condition is not made
of days, so waiving one does not reduce it.

The scaling table is data, in WEEKLY_COUNT_BANDS: (up_to_days_waived,
reduce_by, waives_entirely). 0-2 full count, 3-4 one occasion off, 5-7 not
assessed. Every function takes the bands as an argument, and a test replaces
the whole table with a harsher one and asserts the logic is untouched. Days
outside the table raise rather than being guessed at.

Schema change this card forced (revision b743007f1216): the first migration
allowed one week-scoped instance per chore per week, which is right for a
condition and wrong for a chore due three times a week. Added a `sequence`
column and moved the partial unique index to
(definition_id, week_id, sequence). Verified the batch rebuild left all eight
triggers in place.

sync_week_instances writes a plan to the database. It never touches an
instance the plan still wants, and removes one it no longer wants only if it
is still UNTOUCHED — waiving Monday after the child's claim was confirmed
leaves the claim standing for a parent to decide about.

Tests: 171 pass. The three cases the card asks for — 0, 3 and 6 waived days —
assert 7/2/3, 4/1/2 and 1/0/0 respectively.

2026-08-20 — Session E: claiming, and a parent ruling on claims

app/services/authorisation.py — verify_pin compares the offered PIN against
the configured one with hmac.compare_digest, and returns an Authorisation
naming the party. The party is derived from the credential, never taken from
the request: a client says what it wants done, never who it is. When a second
parent is authorised, this is the one place that changes.

app/routers/dependencies.py — a session per request, committed by the request
itself rather than by the dependency, which is what makes a batch atomic. The
PIN field is a pydantic SecretStr so it cannot be echoed in a repr, a log line
or a validation error.

app/routers/claims.py
  - POST /api/claims — the child, no credential. A claim is a request to be
    believed, not an assertion that money is owed. Refused if the instance is
    already claimed or the week is closed.
  - POST /api/claims/review — a parent's batch, one PIN for the submission.
    The PIN is checked before anything is read; every decision is applied to
    the session and nothing is committed until all of them have applied. Any
    failure rolls the whole thing back and names the item that stopped it.
  - POST /api/instances/{id}/missed — a parent marking a miss without waiting
    for settlement, which is what makes the recovery window usable. Same
    authorisation. Confirmed work is not taken back this way.

A rejected claim returns to UNTOUCHED, not MISSED. Not believing a claim is
not the same as ruling the chore was not done, and an untouched instance stays
provisional until settlement, so the child can still do it.

Schema (revision e42da40283c5): chore_instances.authorised_by, with a CHECK
that a confirmed instance names its author. Alembic does not autogenerate
CHECK constraints on SQLite, so that was written by hand. Two older tests
confirmed work without an author and now say who agreed it.

Verified against a live server, not only the test client: a claim with no
credential returns 200, the same batch with a wrong PIN returns 401 and with
no PIN field 422, and with the correct PIN returns 200 and stores
authorised_by='parent'.

Tests: 196 pass. The three the card asks for — wrong PIN, a batch containing
one inapplicable item, and a valid batch — assert 401 with nothing moved, 409
with nothing moved, and confirmations and rejections applied together.

Not done: there is no attempt limiting on the PIN. A four-digit PIN on a
household LAN can be brute-forced by anything that can reach the port.

2026-08-20 — Session E addendum: rejections leave a trace

A rejected claim returned the instance to UNTOUCHED and cleared claimed_at,
which is the right state, but nothing survived to say the rejection had
happened. From the child's side that is indistinguishable from a tap that
never registered, so he re-claims into the same refusal; and three rejections
left a row reading exactly like one nobody had ever touched.

Revision 7a0c95a307e0 adds chore_instances.rejected_at and rejection_count,
with CHECKs that the count is not negative and that a count above zero has a
time to go with it. Neither column changes a rule — the state is still
UNTOUCHED and still provisional — so the week view can later say "not
accepted, have another go" instead of silently losing the claim.

Claiming again deliberately does not clear either column: claiming does not
unhappen a rejection. A confirmation after a rejection keeps both facts.

Tests: 203 pass, including three rejections on one instance, re-claiming
after a rejection, and a refused batch recording no rejection at all.
